Ministry Of Culture
Scheme for Promotion of Culture of Science: Digital Planetarium/Space And Astronomy Education Centre (Category 1, Category 2 And Category 3)
AstronomyDigital PlanetariumEducation CentreScience CentreSpace
Details
The component "Digital Planetarium/Space and Astronomy Education Centre" under the Scheme for Promotion of Culture of Science by the Ministry of Culture, Government of India aims to create awareness about the sky, space and celestial phenomena, to integrate astronomy education with physical, mathematical and weather science, and to help eradicate superstitions associated with celestial bodies and phenomena. Through this component, financial assistance towards capital cost and corpus fund is provided under three categories to State Governments, Union Territories, and the Societies or Authorities promoted by the State or Union Territory Governments. The Digital Planetarium or Space and Astronomy Education Centre is being added as a new component in the scheme to enhance the Science Centre experience for students and the general public, and it may be co-located in the existing science centres or could be set up in any institution of non-formal learning engaged in science communication activities. The principal objective is twofold, namely to provide a fundamental concept about the celestial bodies, cosmic events and space science, and to alleviate superstitions and misconceptions about planets, stars, eclipses and so forth. The component is implemented by the National Council of Science Museums.
Objectives:
To introduce the sky to the students and the general public, with its innumerable celestial objects and their movement, and thus to create an awareness about astronomy.
To develop an understanding about the sky and the space and about the celestial phenomena.
To integrate the astronomical knowledge with that of physics, mathematics, weather science, artificial satellites and so forth.
To nurture scientific temper by eradicating superstitions related to astronomical objects and events like planets, sun, moon and eclipses.
To correlate our heritage and culture with science.

Conditions Associated with the Benefits:
Under Type B funding, the capital cost for Digital Planetarium/Space and Astronomy Education Centre Category 1, Category 2 and Category 3 is shared 50:50 for all locations other than North East including Sikkim, and 90:10 for the North Eastern Region including Sikkim. The corpus fund is shared 20:80.
The Digital Planetarium could be operated and managed under Type A, Type B and Type C, as detailed for science centres.
Under Type C, the State Governments and Union Territories shall fully fund the project and set it up with technical support from the National Council of Science Museums against payment of consultancy charges.
The corpus fund shared by the Government of India shall in no case exceed 20 per cent of the total Corpus Fund; the principal shall in no case be utilized for any activity; not more than 85 per cent of the interest can be utilised to meet the operational deficit after inauguration, and a minimum of 15 per cent is to be added back to compensate the inflation on a year to year basis.
The share of corpus fund of the Government of India and the State Government or Union Territory is to be released upfront after approval of the project and prior to commencement of the works.
The recurring expenditure will be completely borne by the State Government. Every year, provision for the annual recurring expenditure for maintenance of the equipment and organizing new planetarium shows shall be made by the State Government.
For Hilly Region, Island Territory and North Eastern Region, 20 per cent extra on the entire Capital Cost has been considered.
The building construction cost is considered based on normal foundation. If any special foundation is required to be done based on the Soil Test Report, the additional cost will have to be borne by the State Government.
Escalation of project cost shall be governed by successive cost index over DPAR-2019 as published by the Central Public Works Department at the time of the project work; the extra project cost on this account is to be borne by the State Government or Local Authority.
Content and Facilities Provided:
Category 1: Main Opto-mechanical Star field Projector capable of projecting 8,500 stars down to 6.5 magnitude on a tilted or horizontal Projection Dome; the Projector will have Space or Earth mode system, or a Full dome digital planetarium projector system, or both, depending upon available fund. Dome Size 12 metre diameter; Seating Capacity 120 to 125 unidirectional. The capital cost includes Civil and allied works ₹4.02 crore; Chairs ₹0.15 crore; Air Conditioning of Planetarium theatre and exhibition areas only ₹0.50 crore; Carpet in planetarium theatre ₹0.06 crore; Firefighting System ₹0.25 crore; Pure Digital Full-dome solution with High Contrast Light Emitting Diode or Solid State light source projection system with effective resolution of 4.5K after blending, including cost of 12.0 Metre Diameter inner Aluminium Dome ₹10.00 crore; Uninterruptible Power Supply ₹0.15 crore; Telescope and accessories for sky observations ₹1.00 crore; Office furniture, furnishing and tools ₹0.25 crore; Exhibits on Astronomy within the circular passage around the planetarium theatre with exhibition space of 350 Square Metres approximately ₹1.00 crore; Transportation, Installation and Commissioning of Exhibits at site ₹0.20 crore; Travelling Allowance or Daily Allowance, Training and Miscellaneous Expenditure ₹0.20 crore; and Architects Fee ₹0.22 crore.

Additional Facilities for Category 1 and Category 2: Interactive exhibition on astronomy and space science; Activity area for students on astronomy; Sky observation through a Global Positioning System-enabled telescope; Lecture hall for astronomy lectures by specialists; and Office, maintenance workshop, and show development room.
Additional Facilities for Category 3: Activity area for students on astronomy; Sky observation through a Global Positioning System-enabled telescope; Lecture hall for astronomy lectures by specialists; and Office, maintenance workshop and show development room.
Mode of Disbursement:
Normally, the digital planetarium will be set up by the National Council of Science Museums on a turn-key basis, including construction and commissioning of the equipment, and handed over after completion to the State Government or Union Territory. The construction work shall start only after the share or funding is received from the State or Union Territory Government. Preferably, the construction of the building shall be undertaken by the State Government, Union Territory or agency within the allocated budget as per inputs and supervision by the National Council of Science Museums.
Frequency of Disbursement — Year-wise Utilization of Capital Expenditure:
For all three categories, the capital expenditure is released at 60 per cent in the first year and 40 per cent in the second year.

Expected Time of Completion:
Completion time from the date of issue of the Letter of Intent for the construction work is 24 months for Category 1, Category 2 and Category 3.
The project can be completed within 24 months time from the date of commencement of construction for Category 1, Category 2 and Category 3.
Recurring Expenditure and Projected Revenue:
Category 1: Recurring expenditure of ₹50,00,000 per annum, comprising ₹30,00,000 salary, ₹15,00,000 maintenance and ₹5,00,000 activities and spares approximately, for operating the planetarium, shall be borne by the agency or society running the Planetarium or by the State Government. Considering 60 per cent occupancy, that is 60 per cent public and 40 per cent students, and 5 shows a day, revenue from tickets would be ₹19.44 lakh per year from the public (60 per cent of 360 persons a day at ₹30 per person for 300 days in a year) and ₹8.64 lakh per year from students (40 per cent of 360 students a day at ₹20 per student for 300 days in a year), totalling ₹28.08 lakh per year.
Category 2: Recurring expenditure of ₹40,00,000 per annum, comprising ₹25,00,000 salary, ₹10,00,000 maintenance and ₹5,00,000 activities and spares approximately. Revenue from tickets would be ₹13.00 lakh from the public (60 per cent of 240 persons a day at ₹30 per person for 300 days) and ₹5.76 lakh from students (40 per cent of 240 students a day at ₹20 per student for 300 days), totalling ₹18.76 lakh per year.
Category 3: Recurring expenditure of ₹40,00,000 per annum, comprising ₹25,00,000 salary, ₹10,00,000 maintenance and ₹5,00,000 activities and spares approximately. Revenue from tickets would be ₹8.1 lakh from the public (60 per cent of 150 persons a day at ₹30 per person for 300 days) and ₹3.6 lakh from students (40 per cent of 150 students a day at ₹20 per student for 300 days), totalling ₹11.7 lakh per year.
Validity and Post-Sanction Obligations: 
The Digital Planetariums and Astronomy Education Centres should strive to achieve the following over a period of 5 years from opening:
Generate at least 50 per cent of their operating cost.
Achieve an annual visitors footfall of around 1,00,000 for Category 1 (75,000 for the North East Region), around 75,000 for Category 2 (50,000 for the North East Region), and around 25,000 for Category 3.
Organize a minimum of 5 to 10 astronomy education programmes in a year.
 

Eligibility
The applicant should be a State Government, a Union Territory, or a Society or Authority promoted by the State or Union Territory Government.
The applicant proposing a Category 1 Digital Planetarium should propose a location fulfilling the criteria for Science Centre – Category 1, that is, a city or town with a population of 15,00,000 or more, and the planetarium shall have a 12 metre dome.
The applicant proposing a Category 2 Digital Planetarium should propose a location fulfilling the criteria for Science Centre – Category 2, that is, a city or town with a population between 5,00,000 and 15,00,000, and the planetarium shall have a 10 metre dome.
The applicant proposing a Category 3 Digital Planetarium should propose a location fulfilling the criteria for Science Centre – Category 3, that is, a city, town or location with a population of less than 5,00,000, and the planetarium shall have an 8 metre dome.
The applicant proposing a Category 1 Digital Planetarium should provide a minimum of 2.0 acres of land, or 1.0 acre if the Planetarium is located inside the Science Centre Campus.
The applicant proposing a Category 2 Digital Planetarium should provide a minimum of 2.0 acres of land, or 1.0 acre if the Planetarium is located inside the Science Centre Campus.
The applicant proposing a Category 3 Digital Planetarium should provide a minimum of 1.0 acre of land.
The applicant may either collocate the Centre with an existing Science Centre, or attach it to one, or set it up independently in another non-formal educational institution which is engaged in science popularization activities.
The applicant State Government or Union Territory should give a commitment to provide free land and funds as per norms.
The applicant should employ adequate professional staff for the operation and management of the Digital Planetarium.
The applicant should obtain all other statutory clearances and approvals required by the local authorities of the Central Government, State Government, Union Territory Government or other bodies.
The applicant should choose the land in consultation with, and with the approval of, the National Council of Science Museums.
The applicant should not change the land once offered; if any such incident is noticed after release of grant by the Ministry, the released amount shall be recovered from the organisation with the applicable rate of interest.
The applicant should provide provisions for the solar rooftop in the Detailed Project Report, which is mandatory for the implementing agency.
The applicant should ensure that all buildings constructed under the scheme are designed keeping applicable fire safety norms of the location and the National Building Code.
The applicant should form a Registered Society immediately after the release of the fund by the State Governments or Union Territories towards their share of the capital cost of the project.
The applicant should sign a Memorandum of Understanding with the National Council of Science Museums for implementation of the project, if undertaken and implemented by the National Council of Science Museums.
Preference, Priority and Relaxation:
The land requirement is relaxed to 1.0 acre for Category 1 and Category 2 if the Planetarium is located inside the Science Centre Campus.
The Digital Planetarium is to be preferably co-located in the existing Science Centres, science museums or non-formal education institutions that promote science communication, especially astronomy education.
Preferably, the State Government, Union Territory, or agency shall undertake the construction of the building within the allocated budget, with inputs and supervision from the National Council of Science Museums.
The eligibility criteria of population, land, funding pattern, and feasibility and financial viability of the project considering the intangible outcome of the facility in terms of knowledge gain may be relaxed and/or modified in special cases or circumstances by the Honourable Chief Minister, Government of India, as stated in the document.
In the construction of the building, priority or preference may be given to local material, labour and expertise; wherever possible, community participation may be encouraged; use of renewable energy and energy-saving devices may be given top preference.
 

Exclusions
The principal of the corpus fund shall in no case be utilized for any activity.
 

Application Process
Offline
Step 1: Determine the Category 1 with a 12 metre dome at a location fulfilling the criteria for Science Centre Category 1, Category 2 with a 10 metre dome at a location fulfilling the criteria for Science Centre Category 2, and Category 3 with an 8 metre dome at a location fulfilling the criteria for Science Centre Category 3, and confirm the land availability of 2.0 acres, 2.0 acres and 1.0 acre respectively, or 1.0 acre for Category 1 and Category 2 if inside the Science Centre Campus.
Step 2: Decide the funding Type A, Type B (shared 50:50, or 90:10 for the North Eastern Region including Sikkim, with corpus fund 20:80) or Type C (full State funding with technical support against consultancy charges).
Step 3: Select the land in consultation with, and with the approval of, the National Council of Science Museums; the land cannot be changed later, otherwise the released amount shall be recovered with applicable interest.
Step 4: Obtain the Detailed Project Report format at Annexure E, with Annexure E-1 for the Physical Report on Site.
Step 5: Prepare the Detailed Project Report, including the feasibility report, demand survey for the need of the facility at the location, commitment of land free of cost and the recurring cost, projected footfall and expected revenue generation, to determine whether the proposed Digital Planetarium is viable and financially self-sustainable. It will preferably be a hardbound volume in A4 size in portrait alignment; an incomplete Detailed Project Report will render the application liable for rejection.
Step 6: Attach the mandatory documents, along with the filled Annexure E-1 with Site Map, Google Map and Non-Encumbrance Certificate.
Step 7: Submit the proposal with the Detailed Project Report to:
Joint Secretary (Museums)
Ministry of Culture, Government of India,
Shastri Bhawan, New Delhi-110001
Step 8: The proposal shall be considered on the basis of challenge method guidelines, as and when issued by the Government of India, and per the criteria and parameters prescribed for the purpose in the scheme.
Step 9: The Detailed Project Report will be appraised by the Project Appraisal Committee, comprising the Joint Secretary (Museums), Ministry of Culture; Director or Deputy Secretary (Museums), Ministry of Culture; Representative from the Integrated Finance Division, Ministry of Culture; Director General, National Council of Science Museums; and Director, National Council of Science Museums dealing with the Scheme for Promotion of Culture of Science, for further recommendation to obtain approval of the competent authority.
Step 10: Obtain approval from the Government of India for setting up the Digital Planetarium under the scheme.
Step 11: Sign the Memorandum of Understanding between the National Council of Science Museums and the State or Union Territory Government for implementation of the project, if undertaken and implemented by the National Council of Science Museums.
Step 12: Form the Registered Society immediately after the release of the fund by the State Governments or Union Territories towards their share of the capital cost of the project. A representative of the National Council of Science Museums shall be an ex-officio member of this Society or its Governing Council. The Society shall ensure that the Planetarium functions as per the requirements of its objectives without any deviations. The corpus fund share is released upfront after approval of the project and prior to commencement of the works.
Step 13: Normally, the digital planetarium will be set up by the National Council of Science Museums on a turnkey basis, and construction shall start only after the share or funding is received from the State or Union Territory Government. Preferably, the construction of the building shall be undertaken by the State Government, Union Territory or agency within the allocated budget as per inputs and supervision by the National Council of Science Museums.
Step 14: The Registered Society shall complete the recruitment of the required core staff members within 6 months of the release of funds, with the National Council of Science Museums providing professional support to the Executive Council for recruitment and training of staff, the cost of training being borne by the Society. The required staff strength is as follows:
Category 1 and Category 2, total 6 posts: To be recruited and posted within 3 months after the commencement of the project — Curator or Education Officer, Master of Science (1); Education assistant, Bachelor of Science (1); Lower Division Clerk (1). To be recruited and posted during the installation of the equipment in the planetarium — Technical Assistant, Diploma (1); Technicians, Electronics 1 and Electrical 1 (2).
Category 3, total 4 posts: To be recruited and posted within 3 months after the commencement of the project — Education Officer, Master of Science, or Education Assistant, Bachelor of Science (1); Lower Division Clerk (1). To be recruited and posted during the installation of the equipment in the planetarium — Technicians, Electronics 1 and Electrical 1 (2).
Security and Conservancy shall be run on contract. The National Council of Science Museums shall provide an organic link for the activities and upgradation, and will train the staff members recruited by the Society for the Centre.
Step 15: Complete the project within 24 months from the date of issue of the Letter of Intent for the construction work, that is, 24 months from the date of commencement of construction.
Step 16: Monitoring of Digital Planetariums set up as individual Autonomous Societies under Type B and Type C shall be done by an Executive Council set up by the respective State or Union Territory Government, with due representation from the Ministry of Culture, Government of India, the National Council of Science Museums, the concerned State Government, their private or corporate partners if any, and at least five eminent personalities in the fields of education, Astrophysics, Science and Technology, industry and Science Communication, for recruitment of staff, budgeting, planning and execution of the Annual Plan of activities of the digital planetarium. In case the planetarium is set up in the existing science centres, the existing Executive Council shall monitor the working of the digital planetarium.
Step 17: The Digital Planetarium or Space and Astronomy Education Centres will be operated and maintained by a Registered Society formed by the State Government for this purpose. The Planetarium shall be run by an agency or society formed for this purpose or by a suitable Society. Over 5 years from opening, it should generate at least 50 per cent of its operating cost, achieve an annual footfall of around 1,00,000 for Category 1 (75,000 for the North East Region), around 75,000 for Category 2 (50,000 for the North East Region) and around 25,000 for Category 3, and organise a minimum of 5 to 10 astronomy education programmes in a year.
Jurisdiction: All disputes arising out of the scheme shall be subject to the jurisdiction of the Courts of Delhi and New Delhi only.
